Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Do venue darkness, power access, or setup readiness cause last-minute clarification or event delays?

A pre-event readiness checklist that turns the existing venue instructions into customer-confirmed fields and flags missing answers.

What we could measure together

Measure: Events requiring a preventable site-readiness correction (percent of events).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: Staff verifies site safety and suitability; preserve power and setup requirements rather than relaxing them.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Can packing and lighting-configuration planning reduce setup effort while keeping the chosen games and capacity?

A deterministic equipment checklist based on approved package, player count, and game combination; AI only drafts a readable run sheet.

What we could measure together

Measure: Setup labor for a comparable event package (staff-minutes/event).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: Retain staff sign-off, equipment counts, and the site's published game-configuration restrictions.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Can event quotes expose the full travel, labor, consumables, and power cost before a booking is accepted?

An event-cost worksheet with separate staff time, distance, gear, consumables, and generator/facility-power fields; calculate margin only from owner-entered amounts.

What we could measure together

Measure: Owner-supplied cost coverage in an event estimate (percent of estimates).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: No assumed costs or automatic pricing; electricity and fuel stay separate and require measurement before any energy-saving claim.
