Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could a more complete site brief reduce pre-visit back-and-forth for commercial-kitchen service?

A structured site intake sheet for access windows, equipment identifiers, available photos, and the owner's requested service scope.

What we could measure together

Measure: Clarification contacts needed before a visit can be scheduled (contacts/visit).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: The technician approves the scope; AI cannot certify equipment condition, fire safety, or compliance.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Does collecting job photos and notes into a customer service record consume avoidable time?

Sort owner-provided photos by job ID and draft a factual work-record template with a checklist of missing evidence.

What we could measure together

Measure: Time to prepare a complete service record after a visit (minutes/record).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: Preserve original photos and timestamps; a qualified person verifies descriptions and signs any required record.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could recurring service scheduling reduce travel or missed renewal follow-up while keeping service requirements intact?

A read-only planning sheet that groups owner-approved appointment windows geographically and flags renewal dates for review.

What we could measure together

Measure: Travel distance per completed comparable visit (miles/visit).

Start with 10 consecutive eligible tasks, or all tasks in one week; retain exceptions and record the sample size.

Acceptance boundary: Do not change required service intervals, dispatch appointments, or treat a route estimate as measured fuel or electrical savings.
