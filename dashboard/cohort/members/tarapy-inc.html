Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could clearer descriptions help a prospective participant understand what a particular resource or experience offers?

An owner-reviewed offering sheet covering purpose, format, duration, facilitator role, accessibility information, and what is included.

What we could measure together

Measure: Clarification questions before a participant can make an informed choice (questions/inquiry).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Do not infer someone's mental health, life event, or suitability; descriptions must match a real owner-confirmed offering.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

If guided events or travel experiences are offered, can required logistics be confirmed earlier?

A participant-free readiness checklist with source links, responsible owner, last verification date, and unresolved questions.

What we could measure together

Measure: Required logistics items confirmed by the agreed readiness date (percent of required items).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: No booking, payment, promised availability, or professional safety determination; an organizer verifies arrangements.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could voluntary participant feedback be summarized without exposing sensitive personal stories?

A local topic tally from consented, redacted feedback that retains contradictory views and links themes to anonymized source IDs.

What we could measure together

Measure: Review effort per approved anonymous feedback summary (minutes/summary).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: No diagnosis, therapy claims, fabricated testimonials, or individual vulnerability profiles; keep small identifiable groups out of summaries.
