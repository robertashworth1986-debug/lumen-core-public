Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

If a service catalog is being developed, could location, availability, and logistical requirements become inconsistent?

A catalog completeness and freshness checker with draft questions for the service owner.

What we could measure together

Measure: Catalog entries missing an owner-verified logistics field (entries per proposed catalog review).

Define a synthetic catalog and required administrative fields before assuming a live service workflow exists.

Acceptance boundary: Do not imply services are live or clinically appropriate; no diagnosis or wellness-plan recommendation.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

If providers are listed, could the provenance and review date of administrative documents be difficult to track?

A document-status register that links source, date, reviewer, and next administrative check.

What we could measure together

Measure: Provider records without a traceable document source or review date (records per synthetic audit set).

Use fictional records and an owner-defined administrative checklist; mark verification status unknown without a receipt.

Acceptance boundary: This does not verify credentials, licensing, clinical quality, or regulatory compliance.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

If appointments are coordinated, could booking changes be hard to reconcile across participants?

A read-only scheduling reconciliation report with staff-reviewed notification drafts.

What we could measure together

Measure: Unresolved booking-status conflicts (conflicts per synthetic scheduling set).

Test fictional booking, cancellation, and reschedule events against explicit state-transition rules.

Acceptance boundary: No booking changes, messages, or clinical prioritization; do not collect health histories for this test.
