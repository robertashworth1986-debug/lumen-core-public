Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could important preferences or exclusions be scattered across early planning conversations?

A draft event brief that organizes provided preferences, accessibility needs, timing, and budget limits without filling unknowns.

What we could measure together

Measure: Required brief fields needing later clarification (fields per approved brief).

Audit existing anonymized briefs against a planner-approved checklist and count late corrections.

Acceptance boundary: Planner and client confirm the brief; avoid sensitive inferences or invented preferences.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could vendor quotes be hard to compare when their inclusions and timing differ?

An extraction tool that places quote terms in a comparison table and links each cell to its source.

What we could measure together

Measure: Time to produce an approved like-for-like quote comparison (minutes per comparison).

Time current comparisons and count missing terms using the same sanitized quote set.

Acceptance boundary: No bookings, purchases, negotiations, or commitments; planner verifies every extracted term.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could schedule changes fail to reach every dependent task in an event plan?

A dependency-aware checklist that flags affected tasks and drafts a revised run-of-show.

What we could measure together

Measure: Unresolved dependent tasks after a plan revision (tasks per revision).

Replay a small set of historical or synthetic changes against the existing checklist.

Acceptance boundary: Planner owns the final schedule; tool does not message vendors or change reservations.
