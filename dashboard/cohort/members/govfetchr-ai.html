Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could a source-and-version check reduce ambiguity when an ordinance or agenda is replaced?

An offline document manifest distinguishes current, superseded and withdrawn sample documents and tests whether summaries cite them correctly.

What we could measure together

Measure: Published test summaries linked to the current approved source version (percent).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Use public or synthetic documents; no autonomous publication or interpretation of legal obligations.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Would an exception queue make low-confidence OCR easier for staff to review?

A small benchmark compares extracted dates, titles and agenda items against human-labeled public pages and flags uncertain text.

What we could measure together

Measure: Owner-reviewed fields correctly extracted from a fixed document sample (percent).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: No invented text; unreadable content must remain unresolved.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could a resident-task checklist reveal navigation steps that need clarification?

A manual usability worksheet tests a fixed set of public information tasks, including keyboard-only navigation and source access.

What we could measure together

Measure: Test users completing an owner-selected information-finding task (percent).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Do not claim accessibility compliance from this limited check.
