Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could room constraints and client-stated sensory preferences become difficult to trace across design revisions?

A requirement-to-design matrix that links client-approved preferences to drawings and proposed selections.

What we could measure together

Measure: Approved brief requirements without a traceable design response (requirements per room review).

Designer maps each explicit requirement to the current plan and counts missing or disputed links.

Acceptance boundary: No medical diagnosis or treatment claims; record stated preferences without inferring a condition.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could product substitutions change an important lighting, material, or dimensional requirement?

A source-linked specification comparison that flags missing dimensions and changed attributes.

What we could measure together

Measure: Unreviewed specification differences in substitutions (differences per proposed substitution).

Compare existing substitutions with the designer's approved specification using the same product documents.

Acceptance boundary: Designer verifies suitability; no automatic procurement or claims of sensory or clinical benefit.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could drawings, procurement lists, and client approvals refer to different revisions?

A revision register and document-difference summary that surfaces conflicting versions.

What we could measure together

Measure: Revision mismatches at an approval checkpoint (mismatches per project checkpoint).

Check a bounded set of approved project artifacts against a designer-selected revision register.

Acceptance boundary: Keep final design and purchasing authority with the designer and client.
