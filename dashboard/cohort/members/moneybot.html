Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could a lesson or quiz change leave its standard mapping or answer explanation out of sync?

A versioned curriculum checker that flags changed dependencies and cites the relevant source material.

What we could measure together

Measure: Curriculum items with unresolved mapping or answer-key discrepancies (items per reviewed module).

Educators compare a frozen module against the approved curriculum, rubric, and deterministic answer key.

Acceptance boundary: Educators validate mappings; no claim of standards compliance or learning efficacy from this review.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could AI-guided money examples produce inconsistent arithmetic or omit necessary assumptions?

A deterministic arithmetic checker and assumptions checklist around draft educational explanations.

What we could measure together

Measure: Incorrect or assumption-incomplete responses on synthetic exercises (responses per fixed exercise set).

Run the same teacher-approved fictional budgeting exercises through the existing workflow and score against exact answers.

Acceptance boundary: Use fictional examples only; no personalized financial, investment, or credit advice.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could teacher summaries confuse missing activity data with low completion or understanding?

A source-linked class summary that explicitly distinguishes missing records from observed activity.

What we could measure together

Measure: Summary claims unsupported by available activity events (claims per reviewed class summary).

Compare synthetic class-event logs with existing summaries; track missing-data handling and rubric errors separately.

Acceptance boundary: No autonomous grading or student ranking; use deidentified or synthetic data under educator control.
