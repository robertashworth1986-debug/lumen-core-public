Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could families need several exchanges to determine which program and application route applies?

An approved-program FAQ and form checklist that drafts routing explanations with source links.

What we could measure together

Measure: Administrative clarification exchanges before routing (exchanges per inquiry).

Review an approved sample of deidentified inquiries and record correct program routing, including unresolved cases.

Acceptance boundary: Staff approves eligibility and enrollment; use synthetic examples first and avoid profiling children.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could capacity, schedules, and waitlist status be difficult to reconcile between program records?

A read-only reconciliation sheet that highlights conflicting counts and missing timestamps.

What we could measure together

Measure: Unreconciled capacity or schedule entries (entries per weekly review).

Compare the same dated enrollment, schedule, and capacity snapshots using existing staff rules.

Acceptance boundary: No automatic enrollment decisions or changes to staff-to-child constraints; authorized staff resolves conflicts.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could producing consistent family notices across programs take repeated editing?

A template assistant using approved dates, program policies, and staff-reviewed translation drafts.

What we could measure together

Measure: Staff editing time per approved family notice (minutes per notice).

Time drafting and review of the same notice types; record factual and translation corrections separately.

Acceptance boundary: Staff verifies every notice; exclude student identifiers from general-purpose AI tools.
