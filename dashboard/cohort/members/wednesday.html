Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Can campus requests be grouped into actionable themes without losing less common needs?

Compare a thematic summary of synthetic feedback with a staff-created reference list, including rare themes.

What we could measure together

Measure: Staff time to review all eligible feedback with missed-theme count (minutes/review batch).

Freeze eligible work, observation window and acceptance criteria with the owner; retain incomplete work and exceptions.

Acceptance boundary: Do not infer mental-health conditions or student risk; staff review every proposed theme.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Would current resource information reduce manual checks before staff direct someone to support?

A dated resource checklist records owner, availability source, eligibility and next verification date.

What we could measure together

Measure: Outdated resource references per reviewed directory sample (references/sample).

Freeze eligible work, observation window and acceptance criteria with the owner; retain incomplete work and exceptions.

Acceptance boundary: No automated eligibility or benefit denial; confirm each resource with its responsible campus office.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Which support handoffs lack an acknowledged next owner or closure reason?

A synthetic ticket ledger makes acknowledged ownership, next step and outcome fields explicit.

What we could measure together

Measure: Unacknowledged handoffs beyond a staff-approved target (handoffs/window).

Freeze eligible work, observation window and acceptance criteria with the owner; retain incomplete work and exceptions.

Acceptance boundary: No real student records, confidential stories or disciplinary decisions; closure must reflect actual service, not a cleared queue.
