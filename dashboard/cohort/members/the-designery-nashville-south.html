Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could consultation requests arrive with enough project details and the correct appointment expectations?

A draft confirmation checklist for appointment policy, room type, available measurements, desired materials, and project timing.

What we could measure together

Measure: Consultations needing avoidable scheduling or brief clarification (percent of consultations).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Local staff must resolve the published appointment-policy inconsistency; do not promise walk-in availability, prices, or dates.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could approved measurements and product selections stay synchronized from design through ordering?

A local selection register linking approved drawing revision, measurement verification, SKU, finish, quantity, and client approval.

What we could measure together

Measure: Orders with an owner-confirmed selection or revision mismatch (mismatches/100 orders).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Designers verify measurements and suitability; never send orders or substitute materials automatically.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could a materials-readiness check reduce installation rescheduling caused by missing or inconsistent items?

A checklist comparing the approved bill of materials with confirmed delivery and installer prerequisites before the agreed readiness date.

What we could measure together

Measure: Installations rescheduled for a verified materials-readiness issue (percent of installations).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Do not infer supplier availability, relax construction requirements, or treat every delay as preventable; installers retain professional judgment.
