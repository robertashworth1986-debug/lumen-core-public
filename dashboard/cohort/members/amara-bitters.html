Three questions worth exploring

PUBLIC RESEARCH

These are hypotheses to discuss, with current baselines unmeasured. They do not establish that a problem or loss occurs in this business.

01OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Would a lot-and-expiry inventory view make small-batch replenishment easier to plan?

A local inventory worksheet links SKU, lot, expiry, stock on hand and owner-entered lead time to a review-only reorder flag.

What we could measure together

Measure: Orders delayed by unavailable stock (percent).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Use confirmed inventory; do not predict shelf life or product safety.

02OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Could an approved-answer library reduce repeated nonclinical questions about ordering and product formats?

A source-linked reply library drafts answers only from owner-approved shipping, ingredients and product-format text.

What we could measure together

Measure: Staff minutes spent on repeated shipping or product-format questions (minutes/week).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: Escalate medical, pregnancy, interaction and symptom questions; generate no treatment advice.

03OWNER INPUT NEEDED

Would a pick-and-pack checklist reduce avoidable errors across product sizes and bundles?

A simple order checklist compares ordered SKUs and quantities with a manually confirmed packed list.

What we could measure together

Measure: Owner-confirmed picking errors per fulfilled order (errors/order).

Agree eligible work, observation window, quality threshold and exclusions with the owner before comparing.

Acceptance boundary: No automatic refunds or order edits; retain every exception for review.
