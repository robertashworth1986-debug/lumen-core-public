# LumenCore 30-Day Bounded Validation Sprint

**Status:** Proposed launch offer for founder and buyer review  
**Owner:** Robert Ashworth / LumenCore  
**Commercial role:** Convert one technical claim into one controlled, inspectable decision.

## The buyer problem

AI, forecasting, routing, optimization, and infrastructure teams often have a promising candidate but no clean answer to a basic question:

> Does it perform better than the accepted alternative under rules that were fixed before anyone saw the result?

Internal demos are easy to tune after the fact. Vendor dashboards rarely preserve failures, source rights, comparator selection, configuration drift, or the exact claim that a reviewer is allowed to repeat. That makes procurement, investment, grant review, technical diligence, and pilot approval slower and riskier.

## The LumenCore offer

LumenCore runs a fixed-scope validation sprint around one buyer-owned decision. Before scoring begins, the buyer and LumenCore identify:

1. the authorized source or dataset;
2. the incumbent or accepted baseline;
3. one primary metric and acceptance threshold;
4. the held-out window or frozen simulation seeds;
5. failure, incomplete-run, and rejection rules;
6. what the result may and may not be used to claim;
7. the person who owns the final go/no-go decision.

Candidate and baseline are then evaluated under the same frozen contract. Positive, neutral, incomplete, adverse, and failed results remain in the record.

## What the buyer receives

- a source and data-rights label;
- a pre-registered baseline, metric, threshold, and holdout contract;
- input, code, dependency, and output manifests;
- baseline-versus-candidate replay, benchmark, or simulation results;
- a failure and negative-result register;
- SHA-256 custody records;
- offline verification instructions;
- a buyer-readable Proof Capsule;
- one bounded decision: `promote`, `rerun`, `external review`, `hold`, or `reject`;
- an executive briefing on what changed, what did not, and what evidence is still missing.

## Thirty-day sequence

### Days 0–3 — Decide and authorize

- identify the buyer decision owner;
- confirm source ownership and permitted use;
- select one candidate and one accepted baseline;
- name prohibited claims and data-handling restrictions;
- stop if no lawful, decision-useful evaluation can be defined.

### Days 4–7 — Freeze the contract

- lock one primary metric and threshold;
- freeze the held-out window or simulation seeds;
- define failure and incomplete-run rules;
- record code revision and dependencies;
- approve the evaluation plan before scoring.

### Days 8–17 — Execute and preserve

- verify source integrity;
- run baseline and candidate under matched conditions;
- retain logs, exceptions, constraint violations, negative findings, and missing evidence;
- produce input and output manifests;
- calculate SHA-256 custody records.

### Days 18–24 — Challenge and rerun

- give the reviewer the verification instructions;
- perform the included controlled rerun, when applicable;
- compare internal and reviewer results;
- document discrepancies rather than smoothing them away.

### Days 25–30 — Decide

- issue the Proof Capsule;
- separate measured, replay, synthetic, modeled, estimated, and externally validated statements;
- brief the decision owner;
- record `promote`, `rerun`, `external review`, `hold`, or `reject`;
- scope a later pilot only when the buyer owns the next acceptance contract.

## Entry requirements

The sprint starts only when the buyer can provide or approve:

- a lawful source with a named rights owner;
- a candidate that can be evaluated without unsafe production control;
- an incumbent or agreed naive baseline;
- a decision owner;
- a primary metric that matters operationally;
- written acceptance of the sprint’s claim and IP boundaries.

The standard public offer does not accept classified information, CUI, PHI, payment-card data, export-controlled technical data, production credentials, or autonomous physical-control access. Any such environment requires a separate authorized security, legal, data-handling, and contracting path before information is transferred.

## What this sprint does not promise

A favorable result is not promised. The sprint does not establish guaranteed ROI, realized savings, certification, agency endorsement, award likelihood, production readiness, field validation, customer deployment, safety approval, or autonomous operating authority.

A failed or neutral result may still be valuable because it can stop an unsafe or uneconomic pilot before the buyer spends more money.

## Founder and IP boundary

LumenCore and the buyer each retain their pre-existing code, data, models, methods, documentation, names, and intellectual property. Buyer data remains controlled by the buyer or named rights owner. Rights in buyer-specific outputs must be stated in the signed scope. No transfer, joint ownership, public reference, logo use, or validation claim is implied by a discussion or evaluation.

## Best first use cases

The strongest initial use cases are narrow, replayable decisions such as:

- utility or infrastructure forecasting against an accepted forecast;
- data-quality or anomaly-validation methods against an incumbent rule set;
- AI-agent or workflow reliability under fixed failure conditions;
- routing or scheduling candidates against a named dispatch baseline;
- model-to-source consistency and review-burden reduction;
- technical diligence for a startup, investor, lab, prime contractor, or procurement team.
