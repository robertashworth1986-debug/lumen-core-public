# Founder IP and External Review Boundary

**Project:** LumenCore / Luma Universe  
**Founder:** Robert Ashworth  
**Purpose:** Define how outside reviewers, mentors, investors, service providers, grant contacts, and pilot partners should interpret access to this repository and related materials.

---

## 1. Founder-owned pre-existing architecture

LumenCore and the Luma Universe are founder-originated architecture, naming, evidence, and system-design assets.

This includes, without limitation:

- LumenCore,
- LumaTrader,
- LumaScout,
- LumaJet,
- LumaSuit / LumaSkin,
- FlowForm,
- LumaSpiral / LumenSpiral,
- EtherFrame,
- AetherReach,
- LumenShell,
- EchoForm / Identity Architecture,
- EchoLock,
- proof-to-pilot workflow,
- Proof Capsule schema,
- evidence manifests,
- lexicon and constants,
- prior code, documents, diagrams, prompts, reports, dashboards, and invention disclosures.

No outside party receives rights to these pre-existing assets merely by reviewing materials, joining a call, writing comments, proposing a sprint, contributing feedback, or performing scoped services.

---

## 2. Acceptable outside roles

External parties may be useful as:

- technical reviewers,
- grant/SBIR/STTR mentors,
- pilot-routing partners,
- investor-introduction sources,
- scoped UI/report contractors,
- data-owner validation contacts,
- lab or university reviewers,
- non-exclusive service providers.

These roles do not imply co-ownership, co-invention, exclusive commercial rights, or authority over the platform.

---

## 3. Written agreement required

A written founder-protective agreement is required before any outside party receives:

- access to private repositories,
- access to private run artifacts,
- rights to reuse non-public code,
- rights to derivative commercial work,
- rights to claim co-invention,
- rights to publish LumenCore materials,
- rights to represent LumenCore externally,
- rights to use LumenCore names/marks,
- rights to customer/pilot data,
- rights to grant submission materials,
- rights to sensitive architecture or trade secrets.

---

## 4. Code-for-equity or in-kind support boundary

If a code-for-equity, in-kind engineering, or accelerator relationship is considered, it must clearly separate:

1. pre-existing founder IP,
2. confidential trade secrets,
3. public-safe review materials,
4. scoped service deliverables,
5. new work product,
6. ownership of new work product,
7. license scope,
8. confidentiality,
9. publicity rights,
10. termination and handoff.

Default position: LumenCore retains ownership of all pre-existing IP, architecture, names, proof materials, and prior code.

---

## 5. Reviewer-safe language

Safe external wording:

> LumenCore is founder-owned and is seeking technical review, pilot routing, grant/investor support, and scoped services. Reviewing or advising does not grant ownership of pre-existing LumenCore IP.

Avoid language that implies:

- an outside party built LumenCore,
- an outside party owns LumenCore architecture,
- an outside party is a confirmed partner without written approval,
- any reviewer has endorsed performance claims,
- any review equals certification or field validation.

---

## 6. Evidence boundary

External review may help strengthen evidence, but review is not the same as validation.

Validation requires agreed scope, data rights, baseline, locked metric, run window, success threshold, and written permission to use the result.

Until then, evidence remains one of:

- measured internal evidence,
- replay evidence,
- synthetic benchmark evidence,
- modeled evidence,
- estimated economic framing,
- conceptual architecture.

---

## 7. Founder line

> LumenCore is not a generic app looking for someone else to own or define it. It is a founder-built system architecture with proof assets, module families, and patentable structure. External parties may help accelerate review and pilot access, but the platform spine remains founder-owned.
