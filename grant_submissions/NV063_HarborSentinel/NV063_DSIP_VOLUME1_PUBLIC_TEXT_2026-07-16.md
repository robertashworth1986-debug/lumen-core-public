## Technical Abstract

HarborSentinel is an explainable, low-storage pattern-of-life analysis concept for identifying anomalous surface and air contacts in congested maritime environments. The Phase I effort will assess whether compact, source-aware state can support operator review without requiring a large region-specific archive. Automatic Identification System, Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast, and notional radar or composite-track observations will be normalized into replayable records that preserve source identity, freshness, uncertainty, and observation provenance.

Candidate methods will evaluate route deviation, unusual kinematics, loitering or orbit behavior, source loss, and disagreement among cooperative and non-cooperative observations. HarborSentinel will keep source-integrity review separate from behavior-based threat candidates so a disabled or missing transmitter is not treated as hostile by itself. Each alert will identify the track, anomaly category, supporting observations, source status, machine reasoning, confidence, uncertainty, and plausible benign explanations for watchstander review.

Phase I will deliver an unclassified software prototype, data-adapter specifications, compact pattern-of-life state, an explainable alert schema, a frozen evaluation harness, a failure register, and a notional Ship Self-Defense System integration concept. Feasibility will be evaluated with generated multi-source scenarios, frozen public maritime data where permitted, and government-authorized assumptions or data if provided. Metrics will include detection quality on labeled or controlled cases, review burden on unlabeled traffic, delay, calibration, runtime, memory, storage, and latency by traffic-density and source-health regime.

Existing internal work supports feasibility only. It includes a frozen generated multi-source benchmark and a public NOAA Automatic Identification System held-out split with controlled kinematic injections and named single-axis baselines. Those results do not establish real adversary detection, Navy sensor performance, Ship Self-Defense System integration, operational suitability, or field validation. The Phase I objective is to determine where the approach is technically useful, where it must abstain or defer to an operator, and what data, security, and integration work would be required for Phase II.

## Anticipated Benefits And Potential Commercial Applications

HarborSentinel is intended to reduce the burden of reviewing dense maritime and air traffic while preserving the evidence behind each machine-generated alert. The Navy benefit is a bounded, explainable method for identifying contacts or conditions that warrant operator attention across changing operating areas without depending on a massive historical pattern-of-life database. Compact per-track and local-regime state may reduce storage requirements, improve portability across regions, and make each result easier to reconstruct during technical review.

The proposed Phase I work supports three Ship Self-Defense System needs identified by the topic: maintaining a common tactical picture, helping operators participate in resolving identification conflicts, and using available data to support identification and classification decisions. HarborSentinel will not make autonomous engagement decisions. It will produce advisory evidence for human review and a notional interface-control and display concept for future authorized integration.

The first transition path is Navy evaluation of the unclassified prototype, alert schema, representative-data results, storage and latency profile, and Phase II security and integration plan. A later Phase II effort could use Government-provided architecture information and authorized data to develop and evaluate a prototype for realistic 360-degree Ship Self-Defense System use cases. Any classified, controlled, export-restricted, or operational work would occur only under the Government-approved data, facility, personnel, cybersecurity, and integration boundaries required for that phase.

Dual-use applications include port and terminal security, commercial fleet operations, shipping and logistics, fisheries and environmental monitoring, event and perimeter security, maritime insurance analytics, and critical-infrastructure monitoring. These users face similar problems: high traffic density, incomplete cooperative broadcasts, changing local regimes, limited analyst attention, and a need to explain why an automated system raised an alert. The commercial entry product would be an evidence-bounded anomaly-review service or software component evaluated beside an incumbent workflow on customer-authorized historical data. Adoption would depend on measured review burden, detection quality on adjudicated cases, latency, storage, source rights, and integration cost rather than unsupported performance promises.
