## One-Page Heilmeier Spine

| Question | Best current answer |
|---|---|
| What are you trying to do? | Build a local adaptor architecture that lets heterogeneous AI agents self-organize through sparse peer task markets while local inference controllers expose measurable role-coherence and mission-alignment bounds. |
| How is it done today? | Centralized orchestration, workflow graphs, and ad hoc agent teams often rely on brittle global planners, dense communication, static prompts, or unbounded emergent behavior. |
| What is new? | TA1 coordination and TA2 local control are coupled: coordination depth depends on finite coherence bounds, and control targets are derived from current task contracts and mission constraints. |
| Who cares? | DARPA/DoW users who need resilient long-horizon agent teams in contested, partially observable, failure-prone environments. |
| What is the risk? | Synthetic results may not transfer to foundation-model agents; collusion can bypass local checks; control can suppress useful novelty; compute cost can dominate. |
| How long and how much? | 36 months; current ROM planning estimate is $4.92M, subject to rate, team, cloud, and award-instrument validation. |
| How is success measured? | Mission success, message count, recovery messages, role coherence, alignment, false rejection, compromised assignment, agility/diversity, latency, and inference cost against centralized and decentralized baselines. |
| What are the midterm/final exams? | Phase 1 comparison against centralized SOTA MAS, Phase 2 adversarial/team playoff, and Phase 3 scale/transition evaluation under TA3 infrastructure. |
